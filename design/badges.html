The Badge Book

An achievement system that is actually a curriculum. Each badge is a concept in biology, and the set you have to assemble to earn it is the lesson. An animal that demonstrates four concepts appears in four badges — the overlap is the point, not a mistake.

The Black Cat teaches the player what a badge is

The black cat is a coat option on House Cat, not a species — so this badge needs no catching at all. It fires off a choice the player already made in the first ten minutes, which is exactly what lets it carry the tutorial.

The order is the point. The player gets “black cats are feared for no reason” and then “that was a badge, and here is what badges are.” A tutorial that opens with the achievement menu is a menu. One that opens with a fact about the animal in your arms is a hook.

And the fact is real: black cats are adopted less and euthanised more than cats of any other colour. Superstition with a measurable body count, in a house pet — the same lesson as the aye-aye and the vulture, carried by an animal the player chose to love before the game had asked anything of them.

› choose the black coat  →  fires immediately
› or read the House Cat codex entry  →  fires
› or neither, by the end of the tutorial  →  the badge lights up on its own
The hard one · 24 members · tiered

Bad Reputation

Every other badge teaches a mechanism. This one teaches that fear has a body count — and it is the only badge in the fifty whose lesson is about the player rather than the animal. It covers three separate reasons an animal gets feared, which is why it is large enough to tier.

Twenty-four, not twenty-five. The three lists below add up to 25, but the aye-aye appears twice — feared for its face and killed as an omen. It is one animal, so the badge is 24, and gold sits at 24. Uncle Albert caught that; the earlier count was wrong.

Feared for the name · 8

  • Vampire Squid — its name means “vampire squid from hell”. It eats marine snow, which is detritus
  • Hellbender — also snot otter, devil dog, Allegheny alligator. A harmless salamander
  • Basilisk Lizard — named for a monster whose gaze killed
  • Thorny Devil — eats ants
  • Tasmanian Devil — named for the screaming
  • Giant Oceanic Manta — sailors called it the devilfish
  • Zombie Worm · Goblin Shark

Feared for the face · 9

  • Maned Wolf — at night, a red silhouette on impossibly long legs. Neither wolf nor fox, and it eats mostly fruit
  • Anglerfish — most are under 20 cm. The nightmare image everyone has seen is a magnified photograph
  • Blobfish — it only looks like that because of decompression. At depth it is an ordinary fish
  • Giant Moray — it opens and closes its mouth to pump water over its gills. It is breathing, and every diver reads it as threat
  • Fangtooth — 16 cm long
  • Aye-aye · Gulper Eel · Giant Isopod · Pacific Hagfish

Feared by folklore · 8

  • Black Cat — the tutorial badge, and the one with shelter statistics behind it
  • Aye-aye — killed on sight in parts of Madagascar as an omen of death
  • Rüppell’s Vulture — stomach acid near pH 1 destroys anthrax and botulism, which is exactly why India’s vulture collapse caused a rabies crisis
  • Spotted Hyena — the scavenger reputation is backwards. Lions steal from them more often than the reverse
  • Common Vampire Bat — shares blood with roostmates who failed to feed, and remembers who helped it
  • Barn Owl — an omen across Europe, and the best rodent control a farmer can have
  • Grey Wolf · Common Raven
It loops back into a quest. A player who earns this meets the vulture argument twice — once as prejudice, and once as the mountains quest, where 99% of South Asia’s vultures died to diclofenac and human rabies deaths rose behind them. The badge is why that quest makes sense.
