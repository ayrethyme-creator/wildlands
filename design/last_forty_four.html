The Last Forty-Four

The forty-four species that complete the twelve biomes of Terrane, the badge system reconciled against them, and the rule that finally makes the thousand add up: no cub, pup, caste, morph or sex difference occupies a slot.

Counts read from the Terrane Roster, which reads them from the running game. Every candidate checked against all 1,138 roster entries and all 52 badges. Nothing here is Critically Endangered or Extinct in the Wild, which by the admission rule belongs to On the Brink instead.

Executed · 29 August 2026

This was carried out, and the thousand is closed

The plan below is done. Forty-two of the forty-four were transcribed into design/PENDING_MOVES.txt — the other two were already in the pipeline. Every biome landed on the target named here, the twelve sum to 650, and with The Kept at 50 that is 700. With the 300 already complete: 1000. Uncle Albert reports a shortfall of zero and Cousin Bob passes.

The badge side closed too. All six remaining stars are gone, so the badges reference 324 species, of which 324 exist — nothing on either list is waiting to be invented.

Six things below are superseded. The text is left as written, because this is the record of a proposal rather than a description of the data:

  • It says 52 badges. There are 50. Ayr cut Armour and Rare Poison on 2026-08-28, after this was published. So the Three-banded Armadillo → European Pill Millipede ruling is void, and European Mole keeps its farmland slot on its own merit rather than for Rare Poison.
  • The seven species cut “to hold the number” were never in the roster. Harbour Seal, European Green Crab, Day Octopus, Cactus Wren, Ring-necked Pheasant, Mallard and Water Vole return zero hits against the data — that trade was against an earlier candidate list. Nothing was cut for them, and the Water Vole veto was moot.
  • Forest went differently. The second forest slot went to Wood Frog, not the Indian Giant Squirrel — Sub-Zero needed it, so the proposed Wood Frog → Antarctic Toothfish swap was never taken. The squirrel is in anyway: Black Cockatoo was cut to make room, being five different birds under one name while Sulphur-crested Cockatoo already held that slot.
  • “Nine are marked badge” — it is twelve, and eleven were outstanding once Bumblebee Bat is set aside. All eleven are now in design/BADGES.txt.
  • The two generic renames went the other way. This proposed Chacoan Peccary → Peccary and Edible Dormouse → Dormouse. The repo did the reverse — generic to specific — which is what the naming rule actually requires.
  • Two badges now stand at three members. Ayr ruled that Dad's Turn holds at three rather than invent a fourth male-pregnancy species; Heat Vision fell to three the same way when Green Tree Python was dropped beside the Ball Python already in it.

The deep-sea naming pass was applied in full: 21 renames and five entries keeping the name everyone knows with the binomial credited in the entry. One is flagged for review — Tardigrade → Water Bear is the only rename in the pass that does not clearly gain specificity.

Where the forty-four go

The floor matters and the ceiling does not: a biome with too few species is not an ecosystem, but a big biome holding more than its neighbours is true to the world. Nothing goes to Rainforest, Deep Sea, Polar or Mountains. Every biome finishes at 45 or above, and the twelve land on exactly 650.

The forty-four

Each entry names the job it does in the composition rule — apex predator, mesopredator, herbivore large and small, scavenger, invertebrate, bird — and the fact that makes it worth a card. Nine are marked badge: they were approved off the Badge Book and earn their slot twice.

Open Ocean — The Blue

28 → 45

The middle of the food web is missing. Of 28 entries, ten are whales and dolphins and seven are sharks. Not one schooling fish, one invertebrate, or more than a single seabird — a highlight reel rather than an ocean.

  • Atlantic Bluefin TunaWarm-blooded, crosses the Atlantic in weeks, and its fishery is the textbook case of a quota argued over for forty years
  • Chub MackerelThe schooling fish nearly everything else in this biome is already eating
  • Atlantic HerringThe biomass the North Atlantic runs on; shoals have been measured in cubic kilometres
  • Mahi-mahiGrows faster than almost any fish alive, and the colour drains out of it within minutes of death
  • OpahThe only fish known to be fully warm-blooded — it heats itself by flapping its pectoral fins
  • Oceanic Whitetip SharkWas among the most abundant large animals on earth, and is down by more than 98% in some seas
  • Blue SharkThe widest-ranging shark there is, and the one that follows ships
  • RemoraRides the sharks already in this biome; its dorsal fin evolved into a suction plate
  • Sargassum FrogfishLives only in floating weed and is invisible inside it — the Sargasso is habitat, not scenery
  • Humboldt SquidHunts in coordinated groups and signals to the others by flashing colour
  • Portuguese Man o' WarNot one animal but a colony of four kinds of specialised individual that cannot live apart
  • Northern GannetDives from forty metres and has air sacs in its face to absorb the impact
  • Sooty ShearwaterFlies a figure-of-eight around an entire ocean every year — about 65,000 km
  • Wilson's Storm PetrelOne of the most numerous birds alive, and it appears to walk on the surface of the sea to feed
  • Red-billed TropicbirdComes to land only to nest on a cliff, and can barely walk when it gets there

Farmland — The Furrows

37 → 46

Nothing the Barn Owl eats. Birds, insects and mid-sized mammals, but no rodents, no amphibian, no reptile — the predators already placed here have no prey base under them. It is also the first biome the player ever walks into.

  • House MouseFollowed people out of Asia and into every building on earth
  • Brown RatThe same story told louder, and the wild counterpart to the Fancy Rat in The Kept
  • Field VoleWhat the Barn Owl actually lives on; its numbers crash on a four-year cycle and everything above it follows
  • Common ToadWalks back to the pond it was born in every spring, across roads, in numbers
  • Grass SnakePlays dead convincingly — mouth open, tongue out, and a smell to finish it
  • Eurasian MagpiebadgeOne of the very few animals to pass the mirror test — Who's That
  • Eurasian SkylarkSings only in flight, and its decline is the clearest single measure of what changed in farming
  • European MolebadgeA hand evolved into a shovel — and saliva that paralyses earthworms, which makes it a venomous mammal for Rare Poison
  • European BadgerbadgeA pasture and hedgerow animal, which is why it belongs here rather than in the wood. Embryonic diapause — it can mate in spring and give birth the following winter. On Pause

House Mouse and Brown Rat are the same species as Fancy Mouse and Fancy Rat

The Kept holds the domesticated forms of both. That is allowed, and the roster already does it deliberately — Pig sits in The Kept while Wild Boar sits in the forest, and they are one species. The rule it must not break is the budgerigar rule: the same form is stocked, never duplicated. Write the pairing into both entries the way Rock Dove and Fancy Pigeon already do it. Worth noting the same trap caught Mallard, which is the wild form of the Domestic Duck already in The Kept — one more reason it was the right cut.

Coast — The Strand

40 → 45

No gull, and nothing fixed to a rock. Zonation is the coast's defining pattern and the roster had none of it. The Ochre Sea Star was here without the mussel bed that makes it the textbook keystone predator.

  • Herring GullReads human behaviour well enough to time its raids, and is declining despite how it looks
  • Blue MusselAnchors itself with threads stronger than tendon and builds the bed everything else lives in
  • Acorn BarnacleGlues its head to a rock for life and kicks food into its mouth with its legs
  • Giant Green AnemonebadgeGreen from the algae inside its own tissue. Completes the tide pool, and carries The Slowest and the clownfish mutualism in Strange Allies
  • Aldabra Giant TortoisebadgeIsland gigantism, a century and a half of life, and a walking pace — The Island Rule, The Old Ones, The Slowest

Reef — The Garden

41 → 46

Forty-one species and not one coral. The reef had its fish, its cleaner, its sharks and the starfish that eats coral — but not the animal that builds the place. A player could complete the biome without meeting the reef itself.

  • Staghorn CoralThe fast-growing reef-builder, and the first thing bleaching takes
  • Brain CoralGrows about a centimetre a year and lives for centuries — one colony can outlast a village
  • Giant ClambadgeFarms algae in its own mantle and can live past a hundred — No Bones About It
  • Pistol ShrimpbadgeSnaps a claw hard enough to boil the water, and shares a burrow with a goby that stands watch — The Colony and Strange Allies
  • Humphead WrassebadgeBorn female, and some become male later — The Switch. One fish can be worth more than the boat that took it

Desert — The Dry

43 → 46

  • Egyptian VulturebadgeThrows stones at ostrich eggs to break them — one of very few birds that uses a tool, for The Toolkit

Sixteen slots left empty

Elephant Shrew · Spiny Mouse · Antechinus · Damaraland Mole-Rat · Guiana Dolphin · Arctic Woolly Bear Moth · Painted Turtle · Glass Catfish · Lord Howe Island Stick Insect · Bermuda Petrel · Common Cuckoo · Common Poorwill · Fat-tailed Dwarf Lemur · Bee Hummingbird · Common Swift · Pygmy Three-toed Sloth.

Each is the only animal that carries its point — the only semelparous mammal, the only hibernating bird, the only hibernating primate, the only electroreceptive placental mammal, the only rodent that menstruates. Those badges run one or two members lighter.

The life-stage rule

No cub, pup, juvenile, caste, morph or sex difference occupies a slot in the thousand. The data stays exactly where it is — the entries are not deleted and the sprites are not lost. They simply stop being counted, and a young form becomes a growth state on its adult rather than an entry of its own.

Not counted, from now on

All 120 life stages, the three coat options, the two merged entries, the 44 cut, and the 13 invented wardens. That is what takes today's 1,000 data entries down to 818 countable species — and it is why the thousand now closes exactly.

Three that pointed at nothing

Glass Eel · Elver · Leptocephalus are stages of a freshwater eel, and there is no Anguilla anywhere on the roster. They come out of the count with the rest, and no European Eel is being made. Leptocephalus could legitimately be reattached to the Giant Moray, which has one.

No new young forms as entries

No Badger Cub for the European Badger, no Fur Seal Pup for the Cape Fur Seal. Sprites where the young genuinely looks different — a walrus calf has no tusks — but the entry is the adult.

The orangutan, resolved without a new species

Flanged Male Orangutan is a maturity state, not an entry, the same call already made on Lioness and the Mole-Rat Queen. The two badges that cite "Orangutan" now cite Sumatran Orangutan — On the Brink is available to badges, and Built Wrong on Purpose already draws its whole membership from the Breeding Centre, so postgame members were never actually barred.

The deep-sea naming pass

Every slot in the thousand now has a name, but twenty-six of those names were a genus, a family, an order or a phylum rather than a species — nearly all of them in the deep sea, which the Species Audit had already flagged as "all mine, all generic, all need naming." Where a real common name exists for the right species it is used; where only the binomial identifies the animal, the binomial is the name, which is what the roster already does with Deepstaria and Bathydevius.

One left alone on purpose: Bigfin Squid

The Magnapinna animals on film have never been formally described, so there is no species to name. Say that in the entry rather than attach a binomial that isn't real — "the longest arms of any squid, and nobody has ever caught one" is a better card anyway.

Coelacanth becomes the West Indian Ocean Coelacanth

Latimeria chalumnae — the 1938 trawler off South Africa. The audit already ruled on this shape when it renamed Hippo to Common Hippopotamus: a two-species group gets named, it does not get an exemption. The Indonesian coelacanth is better as the twist inside the entry than as a second slot — sixty-six million years written off, then one on a trawler in 1938, then another in a fish market ten thousand kilometres away sixty years later. Both badges survive: Unchanged and Back From the Dead are about the lineage, and the lineage belongs to the named species too.

Chambered Nautilus needs no change

Flagged in error. "Chambered nautilus" is the standard common name of Nautilus pompilius, one species — it only looked generic beside the family Nautilidae. Record the binomial in the entry and leave the name alone.
