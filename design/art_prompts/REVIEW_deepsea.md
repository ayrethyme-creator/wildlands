## The lesson this batch taught

**A SIMILE IS AS DANGEROUS AS A NEGATION. The model draws the comparison object.**

```
"like a wadded paper flower"   ->  a rose
"like a small bare tree"       ->  a leafless tree, no coral at all
```

Both were *my* wording. This is the same mechanism the craft notes already record for
*"bat-like ears"* becoming actual bat wings — **naming a thing summons it, whether the
sentence denies it or merely compares to it.**

The rule that follows: a comparison to a **body part or a plain shape** is safe
("like a beard", "like a collar", "like a cone"). A comparison to a **whole object or
organism that could stand in for the animal** is not. Twelve such similes were found
and rewritten across the batches still queued, including a French bulldog whose ears
were *"rounded at the top like a bat's"* — the exact documented failure.
