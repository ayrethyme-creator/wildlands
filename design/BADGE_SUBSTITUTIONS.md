# Badge substitutions — species we already have

**Proposal, 2026-08-28. Ayr rules on each line.**

Every orange chip in The Badge Book is a species scheduled for creation. This document
goes through all of them and asks: **is there an animal already in the roster that carries
the same concept?**

Uncle Albert says the badges reference **81 species that do not exist yet**. Only **44
slots remain in the 700**, so the badges alone over-subscribe the roster. Most of that
gap closes here without creating anything.

Every substitute named below was checked against the live roster. **None is invented.**

---

## 1. Eight stars are not new species at all

**These already exist under a different name.** Uncle Albert cannot catch these — he only
checks that a starred name is absent from the roster, and it is, because the same animal
is filed under another spelling. Every one is a free deletion.

| Badge | Starred as | Already in the roster as | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Shot | Periodical Cicada | **Seventeen-year Cicada** | forest |
| Seeing With Sound | Harbour Porpoise | **Harbor Porpoise** | coast |
| The Fastest | Brazilian Free-tailed Bat | **Mexican Free-tailed Bat** | desert |
| The Impostor | Leaf Insect | **Giant Leaf Insect** | rainforest |
| Made By Us | Domestic Pigeon | **Fancy Pigeon** · **Rock Dove** | breeding · farmland |
| See-Through | Comb Jelly | **Venus's Girdle** | deepsea |
| No Father Required | Smalltooth Sawfish | **Sawfish** | brink |
| The Island Rule | Galapagos Giant Tortoise | **Pinta Island Tortoise** | vigil |

Notes on three of them:

- **Harbor / Harbour Porpoise** is one species, *Phocoena phocoena*. A spelling difference
  is hiding it.
- **Brazilian and Mexican free-tailed bat** are one species, *Tadarida brasiliensis* — and
  it is the animal the speed record belongs to.
- **Leaf Insect** and **Comb Jelly** were both renamed in `PENDING_MOVES.txt` during the
  generics audit. The badge file kept the old names. **This will happen again every time
  a rename lands**, which is an argument for a Cousin Bob check.

**Two are renames rather than swaps**, and both fit the naming programme already underway:

- **Sawfish** → *Smalltooth Sawfish*. It is a bare generic on the audit list anyway, and
  the smalltooth is the one with documented parthenogenesis.
- **Peccary** → *Chacoan Peccary*. Retires the `Chacoan Peccary*` star in **Back From the
  Dead** — and the Chacoan is precisely the Lazarus one, known from fossils before it was
  found alive in 1975.

## 2. One badge can be fixed outright

**`Only In Captivity` has zero members** and is the single failing badge on every Uncle
Albert run. It is written as a rule — *"any five from On the Brink tagged EW"* — against an
`EW` tag that does not exist in the data.

**It does not need the tag.** The roster already holds ten genuinely extinct-in-the-wild
species. Naming them directly fixes the badge today:

**Spix's Macaw · Guam Kingfisher · Socorro Dove · ʻAlalā · Kihansi Spray Toad · Wyoming
Toad · Barbary Lion · Père David's Deer · Northern White Rhino · South China Tiger**

The first six are textbook EW. Three carry a caveat worth knowing: the **Northern White
Rhino** is two females and functionally rather than formally EW; the **South China Tiger**
is listed CR with no confirmed wild animals; the **Barbary Lion** survives only as captive
descendants. **Przewalski's Horse** and the **Scimitar-horned Oryx** were EW and have since
been reintroduced — they belong in *Back From the Brink*, not here, and the contrast is
worth using.

## 3. Badges that need nothing at all

Four badges have enough existing species to reach gold with **no creation whatsoever**.

**Lights On** — bioluminescence. Beyond the six real members, the deep sea already holds
**Lanternfish, Viperfish, Hatchetfish, Stoplight Loosejaw** *(red light — an invisible
torch)*, **Bloody-belly Comb Jelly, Helmet Jellyfish, Bathydevius** and **Giant
Siphonophore**. `Hawaiian Bobtail Squid*` and `Antarctic Krill*` are both optional.

**No Bones About It** — invertebrate giants. **Giant Isopod, Alicella, Xenophyophore** *(a
single cell 20 cm across)*, **Giant Gippsland Earthworm** *(3 m)*, **Goliath Beetle,
Hercules Beetle, Chan's Megastick, Atlas Moth, Amazonian Giant Centipede, Antarctic Sea
Spider, Giant Barrel Sponge, Giant Tube Worm**. `Giant Clam*` unnecessary.

**The Highest** — altitude. **Vicuña** *(and its oxygen-efficient blood)*, **Kiang,
Gelada, Alpine Ibex, Markhor, Takin, Woolly Flying Squirrel, White-rumped Vulture**.
`Himalayan Tahr*` is redundant.

**Not What It Looks Like** — misleading names. **Horseshoe Crab** *(not a crab — closer to
a spider)*, **Mantis Shrimp** *(neither)*, **Vampire Squid** *(neither)*, **Guinea Pig**
*(not from Guinea, not a pig)*, **Turkey** *(not from Turkey)*, **Binturong** *("bearcat")*,
**Sea Pig, Koala, Velvet Worm, European Pill Millipede** *(constantly mistaken for a
woodlouse)*. `Electric Eel*` is a good one but entirely replaceable.

## 5. Worth creating anyway — the ones with no substitute

Cutting all of the above still leaves a short list where the roster genuinely has nothing:

- **Wood Frog** — *Sub-Zero* names it in the concept line itself. It freezes solid, stops
  its heart, and thaws. Already wanted in `HANDOFF.md`.
- **A venomous shrew or the Solenodon** — *Rare Poison* has only **Platypus** and **Slow
  Loris** without one, and **Common Vampire Bat** is arguable at best (draculin is an
  anticoagulant; whether that is venom is contested). This badge is genuinely thin.
- **Termite** — the other great eusocial lineage, and there is no termite anywhere in the
  roster. A real gap, not just a badge slot.
- **Oilbird** — the only non-mammal echolocator available.
- **Bumblebee Bat** — already on the wanted list in `HANDOFF.md`.
- **Black Cat** — deliberate. It is a coat option on House Cat, not a species, and the
  star should probably become a `~` rule so Albert stops counting it.

**Six, against eighty-one.**

Five more come free if the stranded batches land: **Pistol Shrimp**, **Sea Anemone**,
**Giant Clam** (reef), **Salp** (open ocean) and **Himalayan Tahr** (mountains).

## 6. Two errors found on the way through

**`Dad's Turn` has the wrong animal in it.** The badge is male pregnancy, and **Ornate
Ghost Pipefish** is listed as a member. Ghost pipefish are *Solenostomidae*, not
*Syngnathidae* — **the female broods the eggs**, in a pouch formed from her fused pelvic
fins. It is the opposite of the thing the badge teaches. The seahorses and seadragons are
correct; this one is not.

That also means `Common Seahorse*` may be worth keeping after all, since removing the
pipefish drops the badge to three real members.

**The published Badge Book is behind `BADGES.txt`.** The page still shows `Antarctic Krill*`
in *Lights On* and *The Linchpin*, and *Lights On* tiered at 2/6/8 — the file has since
replaced the krill with **Lanternfish** and **Viperfish** and retiered to 3/6/9. The data is
newer than the page. It needs regenerating with `make_badge_page.py`.

**One trap to avoid:** the **Gaboon Viper** looks like an obvious *Heat Vision* member and
is not one. *Bitis* is a true viper, not a pit viper — no pit organs. Use the **Ball
Python** instead.

## 7. The fact check, 2026-08-28

Ayr asked whether the California condor is really parthenogenetic. **It is** — San Diego
Zoo Wildlife Alliance confirmed two chicks, SB260 and SB517, by genetic analysis in the
*Journal of Heredity* in 2021. What makes it the strongest entry in that badge is that
**both mothers had been housed with fertile males and had already bred sexually**; every
earlier vertebrate case involved an isolated female.

The full pass found ten members that were wrong. **All ten are removed.** Four of them
were introduced earlier the same day by the substitution work above, which is worth
recording — a substitution is a biological claim, and picking one from memory is exactly
how the badges got their existing errors.

| Badge | Removed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No Bones About It | **Xenophyophore** | A foraminiferan **protist**. Not an invertebrate, not an animal at all |
| The Trade | **Vaquita** | Not trafficked. It drowns in gillnets set for **totoaba**, which is the trafficked animal and is not in the roster |
| Rare Poison | **Common Vampire Bat** | Draculin is an anticoagulant in saliva. Vampire bats are not classified as venomous |
| Who's That | **African Elephant** | The mirror test was passed by **Asian** elephants (Plotnik 2006). African elephants never have. The Asian elephant was already a member and stays |
| The Island Rule | **Pygmy Hippo** | A mainland West African forest animal in its own genus. The island dwarf hippos were Cretan, Cypriot and Maltese, and all are extinct |
| Heat Vision | **Boa Constrictor** | Most boids have labial pits. *Boa constrictor* does not |
| Heat Vision | **Common Vampire Bat** | It does sense infrared, but through TRPV1 receptors on the nose leaf — not pit organs, which is what the badge is about |
| Strange Allies | **Honey Badger** | The honeyguide/honey badger partnership is **largely unsubstantiated**; attempts to document it have failed. The evidenced honeyguide relationship is with **humans** |
| The Toolkit | **Giant Pacific Octopus** | The tool user is the **coconut octopus**, which assembles shell halves into armour — and it is in the stranded reef batch |

**One species had to be added back.** Removing two members took *Heat Vision* to three,
below the four-member floor, so **Green Tree Python*** returns as a star — it has the
deepest and most numerous labial pits of any snake. Species to create goes 9 → 10.

**One concept line was false and is rewritten.** *The Sixth Sense* claimed *"platypus and
echidna are the only mammals with it"*. The **Guiana dolphin** has electroreception too,
through vibrissal crypts, published in 2011 — and it was a starred member of this very
badge until that morning. It now reads *"barely any mammal has it, and the platypus
carries 40,000 receptors in its bill"*.

### Sub-Zero — **OPEN, awaiting Ayr**

The badge reads *"Antifreeze proteins — the wood frog freezes solid, stops its heart, and
thaws."* **Those are two opposite strategies and the line contains both.** Antifreeze
proteins *prevent* freezing, which is the icefish and the toothfish. The wood frog has no
antifreeze proteins: it *lets itself freeze* and survives it, flooding its tissues with
glucose as a cryoprotectant.

So it is either:

- **antifreeze proteins** — keep Antarctic Icefish, Arctic Cod, Antarctic Toothfish,
  Arctic Char, and the wood frog does not belong; or
- **surviving being frozen** — keep the wood frog, and the fish do not belong.

Ayr is thinking about it. **Nothing has been changed.**

### Contested — known, not acted on

These are not errors, but none is as solid as it reads. Recorded so nobody has to
rediscover them:

- **Common Kestrel** in *Beyond Violet* — the vole-urine-reflects-UV story (Viitala 1995)
  has been challenged and may not hold. The reindeer in the same badge is solid.
- **Anglerfish** in *Lights On* — its light is made by **symbiotic bacteria**, which sits
  awkwardly under a concept line reading *"light the animal makes itself"*.
- **Goliath Grouper** in *The Switch* — protogyny in this species is equivocal in the
  literature, unlike the wrasses and parrotfish beside it.
- **Okapi** in *End of the Line* — Giraffidae has two living members, so it is the last of
  its genus rather than its branch.
- **Orca** in *Who's That* — one 2001 study, never replicated. Weaker than the others.
- **The Old Ones** counts **Black Coral**, **Deep-sea Bamboo Coral** and **Giant Barrel
  Sponge**, which are *colonies*, against a concept line reading *"how long one
  **individual** lives"*.
- **Grounded** says *"flightlessness where nothing hunts on the ground"*, which does not
  describe the ostrich, emu or cassowary — ratite flightlessness has a different cause.
- **The Smallest** — the volcano rabbit loses "smallest rabbit" to the pygmy rabbit, and
  the dik-dik loses "smallest antelope" to the royal antelope. Neither is in the roster.
